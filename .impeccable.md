## Design Context

### Users
People who want a **spectacle toy**, not a policy workstation. They land cold from a link or trend chip, pick a fight topic, watch five famous AIs clash as partisan fighters, crown a winner, and share the verdict in under a minute. Context: phone in hand, group chat energy, “you have to see this.”

### Brand Personality
**Theatrical · Competitive · Sharp**

Emotional goals: adrenaline of a live bout, rivalry of a draft night, payoff of a shareable punchline. Never academic, never enterprise dashboard, never purple “AI product.”

### Aesthetic Direction
**Capitol Night Fight** — prime-time chamber bloodsport.

- Atmosphere: deep ink-navy chamber (tinted, never pure black), brass/gold brand signal, cobalt vs crimson team rivalry, slate independents
- Typography: condensed display for the brand and fight titles; readable serif for argument body; clean geometric sans for UI chrome
- Motion: staggered entrances, score ticks, cast shuffle, share-card reveal — purposeful, not bounce/elastic
- Anti-references: purple-on-dark AI slops, glassmorphism soup, Inter/Roboto defaults, cream+terracotta editorial, broadsheet hairline columns, nested card grids, sterile “mature debate table”

### Design Principles
1. **Brand is the hero** — APICONGRESS must dominate the first viewport; no secondary marketing clutter.
2. **One job per screen** — land → cast → fight → crown → share.
3. **Moments over manuals** — every phase ends with something screenshot-worthy.
4. **Speed is part of fun** — no fake multi-second waits; drama comes from rivalry, not loading.
5. **Share is a product surface** — every finished debate produces a portable artifact (link + X intent + copy card).
6. **Harden for chaos** — long topics, missing APIs, mock mode, mobile thumbs, reduced motion.
